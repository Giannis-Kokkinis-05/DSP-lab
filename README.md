For ex1_1 I had to distinguish telephone tones sampled at 8192Hz. Those tones consisted of two frequencies a low and a high one and lasted for 1000 samples at random locations. The tones could have also been "infected" by noise. In order to tackle this problem I stored the frequency bins of the two peaks of every tone and compared them to the tone I had found. To search for the tones I moved a rectangular window of five samples across the signal calculating its energy and compared it to the bound 5, which I selected while testing!
For ex1_2 we were tasked to process a small phrase signal and a song of 30 seconds in order to distinguish voiced (e.g. vowels, low frequencies) and voiceless sounds (e.g. /sh/, /f/, /p/, high frequencies). This was done by calculating the short time energy, zero crossing rate (plotted on the signal), spectral flux and spectral centroids (plotted on the stft) in windows of 20-50ms duration.
For ex2_2 I had to pass a stereo song through a filter bank consting of 32 frequency bands. Each output of the filters was quantized adaptively using 8 or 9 bits (high bands) based on the global frequency masks my partner provided me with. This was my least favorite exercise since it was only about implementing functions rather than observation. The output results were really good though!
